What it means for you
Inventory just hit its highest point of 2026 and sellers are carrying 5.8 months of supply — and the 30-year fixed just came back down, 7.28% to 7.21% in a week. For once both windows are opening at the same time. Do not let that make you sloppy: underwrite at 7.5%, not at today’s quote, and keep asking for seller-paid closing costs or a rate buydown, which costs a motivated seller less than the price cut you were about to request anyway.
Bring your toughest deal to Launch Night — we’ll underwrite it on the big screen with the room, and the segment goes up in the Facebook group afterward.

Legal & Housing Corner
Your 30-day deposit letter decides who keeps the money
Florida hands you two clocks the day a tenancy ends. Claiming nothing? The deposit goes back within 15 days. Claiming something? §83.49(3)(a) gives you 30 days from termination to send written notice of your intent to impose a claim and the reason for it — by certified mail to the tenant’s last known mailing address, or by e-mail under §83.505 — and the letter has to track the statutory form, including the line telling the tenant they have 15 days to object. Miss that 30-day window and you forfeit the claim outright: no deduction, no setoff, and you can only sue for damages after you have returned the whole deposit. It bites hardest on BRRRR exits and on closings where you inherited a tenant and never saw the move-in paperwork. (Source: §83.49(3), Fla. Stat., 2026 Florida Statutes, current as of Nov 22, 2026.)

Put a day-20 reminder on every move-out in your calendar and file the certified-mail receipt with the lease. Bring a deposit letter you have actually sent on the 21st and we’ll read it against the statute during open networking.
General information for investors, not legal advice. Talk to a Florida real estate attorney about your specific deal.
